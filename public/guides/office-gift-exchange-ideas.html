Office gift exchange: budget, rules and ideas

Updated November 25, 2026

For an office gift exchange, keep it optional, set a budget of $20 to $30, and draw names so no one, including the organizer, sees the pairs. Ask everyone for a one-line wishlist so gifts are useful.

Rules that keep it easy

  1. Make it opt-in. Ask who wants to join before drawing. No one should feel pushed into spending money.
  2. Keep the budget small. $20 to $30 is normal. Say it clearly, and say whether it's a limit or a target.
  3. Draw names privately. Use a link where each person sees only their own match. This matters at work, since the organizer is often a coworker too.
  4. Set the swap day, usually the last team lunch or meeting before the holidays.
  5. Keep gifts work-appropriate. Nothing personal, nothing that needs explaining to HR.

Gift ideas that work for coworkers

BudgetIdeas
Under $20Good coffee or tea, a nice pen, a desk plant, fancy hot sauce, a puzzle, a mug from a local shop
$20 to $30A cozy throw, a book you think they'd like, a quality water bottle, a board or card game, a local food gift box
Remote teamsA gift card for a local coffee shop near them, a food delivery credit, or a book sent straight to their address

The wishlist line is the real trick. "I drink a lot of tea" turns a random gift into a useful one.

Remote and hybrid teams

Draw names with a link in your team chat, set a ship-by date a week before the swap, and open gifts together on a call. Have people ship gifts directly to each other's home or office.
